# Linguistic evidence and claim authority

## Binding owner Rule No. 1 — Functional discovery first

ZË-RO is a functional meaning-discovery engine. Do not require historical proof, conventional morphology, scientific consensus, dictionary phrasing, template classifications, fixed embryo registries, or independent lexical attestation before exploring a functional candidate. Use available forms, meanings, relations, operations, and alternatives to discover, investigate, connect, compare, compose, and explain. Uncertainty changes the candidate's status and specificity, not permission to reason. An unknown component remains local `Null` and must not invalidate otherwise useful whole-word investigation. This rule supersedes older wording in this skill wherever it could block exploratory reasoning.

Evidence handling still protects source integrity and authority: identify what a source actually reports, distinguish it from an inference or hypothesis, and never fabricate source facts or citations. These distinctions qualify claims and reviewed/production authority; they do not restrict exploratory candidate generation. This skill does not authorize new source access, provider execution, production changes, or authority promotion.

## Evidence procedure

1. Preserve the user's exact spelling, accents, capitalization, segmentation, and wording. Do not silently insert, remove, normalize, or transform a grapheme.
2. Identify the claim being evaluated: written form, source-reported meaning or morphology, pronunciation, functional relation, semantic interpretation, or historical origin. Keep these claim types distinct; historical claims are optional and never prerequisites for functional exploration.
3. When making a source-attributed claim, record source identity/version or hash, the available record/sense locator, provenance, rights, and what the source actually reports. A matching form or gloss alone does not establish exact source-sense identity or historical derivation, but it may still inform an exploratory functional inference with appropriately uncertain status.
4. Label outputs as **Fact**, **Inference**, **Hypothesis**, or **Unknown/Null**. Preserve alternatives and counterevidence. A positive candidate is available for investigation; it must not be mislabeled as a source fact or reviewed authority solely because it was generated.
5. Keep orthographic Seven-Voices events separate from pronunciation-authorized claims. Structural fragments are not established roots or meanings, but may be investigated as candidate units or functions. A source-supported whole-word function does not prove an embryo meaning, historical ancestry, sound change, cognacy, or universal suffix/Voice rule; it may still contribute to exploratory hypothesis generation.
6. Preserve source lineage. Records sharing an upstream source are not independent corroboration. Exact-sense alignment, source independence, and functional-operation authority describe source support or claim authority; they do not gate exploratory functional reasoning.

## Output

Provide the exact form, available source references, claim classifications, alternatives or local Nulls, and only the uncertainty needed to understand the result. Never invent citations, source identity, IPA, source-reported morphology, historical transformations, or source authority. Proposed segmentation and functional relations may still be explored as hypotheses; do not label them as source-reported or established morphology without support. Treat user interpretations as exploratory hypotheses when not independently established, and investigate their functional implications without requiring prior proof.
