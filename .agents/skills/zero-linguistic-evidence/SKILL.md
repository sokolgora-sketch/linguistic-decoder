# Linguistic evidence and claim authority

This skill governs evidence handling and claim wording. It does not authorize research execution, new source access, production changes, or new linguistic rules.

## Evidence procedure

1. Preserve the user's exact spelling, accents, capitalization, segmentation, and wording. Do not silently insert, remove, normalize, or transform a grapheme.
2. Identify the claim being evaluated: written form, source-attested meaning, source-reported morphology, pronunciation, functional relation, semantic interpretation, or historical origin. These are separate claims.
3. For each source, record its identity/version or hash, exact record and sense locator, provenance, rights, and the scope of what it attests. A matching form is not exact sense identity; a dictionary gloss alone does not prove derivation or functional composition.
4. Label claims as **Fact**, **Inference**, **Hypothesis**, or **Unknown/Null**. Preserve counterevidence, alternatives, and negative controls. Do not promote a plausible relationship because it produces a positive result.
5. Keep orthographic Seven-Voices events separate from pronunciation-authorized paths. Structural fragments are not automatically roots or meanings. Source-supported whole-word function does not establish independent embryo meaning, historical ancestry, sound change, cognacy, or a universal suffix/Voice rule.
6. Preserve source lineage. Records sharing an upstream source are not independent corroboration. Keep exact-sense alignment, source independence, and functional-operation authority as distinct gates.

## Output

Provide the exact form, source/sense references, claim classifications, alternatives or Null, and the narrowest evidence gap. Never invent citations, source identity, IPA, morphology, historical transformations, or semantic authority. Treat user interpretations as hypotheses unless independently supported.
