## 2. Working title

Working internal title:

> High-Region Pressure, Token Geometry, and Boundary Instability in Cohort 03

Alternate shorter title:

> Cohort 03 Mechanism Note: High-Region Pressure and Token Geometry

## 3. Core thesis

Draft internal thesis:

> Cohort 03 is most useful as a mechanism map. Its strongest contribution is not a clean bracket-support claim, but the identification of pressure mechanisms: high-region anchor instability, target/high-anchor token-geometry mismatch, boundary uncertainty, and length-sensitive stabilization.

This thesis is intentionally internal and cautious.

It does not claim:

- model confirmation;
- model falsification;
- cohort-level publication readiness;
- Hindi `/i/` support for `V6-V7`;
- solved high-region placement;
- Semitic high-vowel support.

## 4. Why this note exists

Cohort 03 contains mixed evidence.

Support-like evidence exists, especially:

- Finnish `/ä/`;
- Finnish `/ö/`.

But Cohort 03 is dominated by mechanism boundaries and pressure cases:

- Finnish `/y/`;
- Semitic `/i/`;
- Hindi `/i/`.

The note should explain why those pressure cases are scientifically useful rather than treating them as failed noise.

## 6. Proposed note structure

### Section 1 — Why Cohort 03 is mechanism-first

Purpose:

- explain that Cohort 03 is not publication-ready;
- frame pressure results as boundary evidence;
- distinguish internal mechanism value from public support claims.

Key points:

- Cohort 03 is a mixed mechanism cohort.
- Support-like cases exist, but they are not the whole story.
- Pressure cases are central to the research value.

### Section 2 — Support-like bridge cases

Purpose:

- summarize the support-like side without overclaiming.

Cases:

- Finnish `/ä/`;
- Finnish `/ö/`.

Draft reading:

> Finnish `/ä/` is the strongest support-like bridge case in Cohort 03. Finnish `/ö/` is weaker but still useful as lower front-rounded bridge evidence. Neither is enough to make Cohort 03 suitable for public release by itself.

### Section 3 — High-region pressure cases

Purpose:

- group the unstable high-region evidence.

Cases:

- Finnish `/y/`;
- Arabic `/i/`;
- Hebrew `/i/`;
- Hindi `/i/`.

Draft reading:

> High-region cases repeatedly expose anchor instability and collapse pressure. These cases should be used to refine mechanism questions, not to claim support.

### Section 4 — Hindi `/i/` as the central mechanism story

Purpose:

- make Hindi `/i/` the worked example.

Evidence sequence:

1. Hindi/Arabic `/i/` comparison;
2. long-`ee` vs short-`i` high-anchor probe;
3. target-split geometry inspection;
4. first length-matched candidate result;
5. length-matched replication result;
6. mechanism synthesis.

Draft reading:

> Hindi `/i/` is length-sensitive and high-boundary stressed. Length matching repeatedly prevents full high collapse at the verdict level, but the replication remains boundary-stressed. This is a mechanism result, not a solved bracket-support result.

### Section 5 — Token geometry as pressure mechanism

Purpose:

- explain why target/high-anchor shape matters.

Variables to track:

- mean token length;
- open-final / closed-final distribution;
- short-`i` marker density;
- max consonant cluster;
- long high-front marker contamination;
- high-anchor lexical shape.

Draft reading:

> Token geometry is not noise. It can control whether a target bucket collapses, stabilizes, or remains boundary-stressed.

### Section 6 — Boundary uncertainty as evidence, not failure

Purpose:

- frame `BOUNDARY_UNCERTAIN_HIGH`, `NEAR_COLLAPSE_HIGH`, and edge stress.

Draft reading:

> Boundary flags should be treated as mechanism evidence. They identify pressure zones where the bracket model is under stress.

### Section 7 — What Cohort 03 allows and blocks

Purpose:

- restate claim boundaries.

Allowed:

- Cohort 03 maps pressure mechanisms.
- Hindi `/i/` is length-sensitive.
- Finnish `/ä/` and `/ö/` are support-like internal cases.
- Finnish `/y/`, Semitic `/i/`, and Hindi `/i/` are pressure cases.
- Boundary instability is a legitimate finding.

Blocked:

- Do not claim Cohort 03 confirms the model.
- Do not claim Cohort 03 is publication-ready.
- Do not claim Hindi `/i/` supports `V6-V7`.
- Do not claim length matching solves Hindi `/i/`.
- Do not claim Semitic `/i/` supports a high bracket.
- Do not update README with Cohort 03 claims.
- Do not publish Cohort 03 to Zenodo or LingBuzz now.

### Section 8 — Next research designs

Purpose:

- convert pressure evidence into narrow future questions.

Candidate future questions:

1. Does open-final versus closed-final distribution explain high-edge pressure?
2. Does short-`i` marker density reduce or increase high-boundary stress?
3. Do front-rounded high-region vowels require a different anchor protocol?
4. Is Semitic high-vowel collapse caused by token shape, anchor choice, or deeper bracket mismatch?
5. Can Hindi `/i/` be stabilized without boundary flags by controlling target shape beyond mean length?
